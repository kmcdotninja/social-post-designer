# Planning the campaign, for any brand

The engine draws whatever posts.js describes. The thinking is here: who the posts are for, what they must make
people do, and which series say it. Do this before writing a single post.

## 1. Read the brief into six answers

| Question | If the user didn't say, infer it from |
|---|---|
| **What is the brand and what does it sell?** | its website, app store page, logo, the repo, the sentence the user gave |
| **Who is it for?** (age, city, income, what they worry about) | the product and the region |
| **What's the moment?** launch, countdown, new feature, promo, event, holiday, always-on month | the user's words ("we're launching", "next month") — default: launch for new brands, always-on for existing |
| **What should someone do after seeing a post?** download, book, call, order, visit, sign up, follow | the business model: app → download; service → call/WhatsApp; shop → order/visit; event → register |
| **Where and how many?** Instagram 4:5 by default; count | default 30 for a month of always-on, 60–100 for a launch with countdown |
| **What must never be said or shown?** | the industry's rules (below) and the brand's own limits (ask if it's faith, health, children or money) |

Ask the user only what can't be inferred and changes the work (contact details, real offers, real numbers, dates).
Never invent prices, rates, discounts, licences, awards or testimonials: leave a visible `[placeholder]` and list
them for the user to fill.

## 2. Pick the campaign shape

- **Launch** (new brand, branch, product): teasers → countdown → launch day → what we do → how it works → proof →
  engagement → places/audiences. 60–100 posts for a full launch; for a small set (~20) keep the same order with one
  post per beat: 2–3 teasers, a countdown post every few days of the lead time (e.g. 14, 7, 3, tomorrow), 2–3 on
  launch day, then features, proof and engagement.
- **Feature drop** (an app ships something): the hero announcement, then one post per benefit, how-to steps, a
  before/after, a release sticker set. 8–16 posts.
- **Promo / sale**: the offer as the poster (the number huge), the deadline countdown, what's included, the terms
  line, a last-chance post. 8–12 posts.
- **Always-on month**: a rhythm, not a story: 2 tips, 1 product, 1 engagement, 1 proof a week, plus the month's dates
  (holidays, paydays, seasons). 20–30 posts.
- **Event**: save the date, speakers/what's on, countdown, practical info (where, when, how to get in), live day,
  thank you. 12–20 posts.

## 3. Turn it into series, and each series into layouts

A series is one idea told several ways, with its own look, so the grid never repeats itself. 8–15 posts per series;
mix 2–4 layouts inside a series; vary the colourway post to post (never the same twice running).

Every series draws from **both** big families — the poster family (`po-`, layouts-poster.md) and the campaign family
(`ca-`, layouts-campaign.md) — with the base family (layouts-base.md) as filler. Alternate them inside a series so
the feed shows both systems side by side.

| Series | Says | Poster family (`po-`) | Campaign family (`ca-`) | Base |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| Countdown | n days to go | po-number, po-numbers, po-glyph | ca-count-big, ca-cd-tile, ca-cd-burst, ca-cd-extrude, ca-cd-window, ca-cd-note, ca-cd-save, ca-count-photo | countdown |
| Launch day | we're here, here's how to start | po-big, po-release, po-photo-full, po-bands | ca-pass, ca-pass-wide, ca-passport, ca-photo-card | hero, photo |

## 4. Industry playbooks

Each: who you talk to, what they need to hear, the series that work, the pictures, and the lines not to cross.

**Fintech, banking, payments, savings, investing, insurance**
- Audience: people who've been burned by fees, failed transfers, hidden charges. They need trust before features.
- Say: speed, safety, no hidden fees, control, small habits that add up. Use real product numbers only if given.
- Series: launch, features (save, send, pay bills, cards, invest), how it works, security ("your money is
  protected" with the lock icon), money tips (budgeting, saving challenges), paydays and month-end, proof (users,
  reviews), referral offer.
- Pictures: the `app` and `card` placeholders → real app screenshots in a phone frame as soon as there are any; hands
  holding phones (trace, see illustrations.md); people at markets, offices, campuses.
- Never: promise returns ("guaranteed 20%"), say "risk-free", imply a licence or deposit insurance you weren't told
  about. Investment posts carry a short risk line if the brand has one. Rates and fees only from the user.
- CTA: "Download {name}" + the website or "on the App Store and Google Play".

**Food, restaurants, delivery, groceries**
- Say: taste, freshness, speed, price, the menu's heroes. Photos carry it: food big, close, bright.
- Series: menu heroes (one dish a post, photo full), deals of the day, new on the menu, behind the kitchen, how to
  order, delivery areas (places), reviews, weekend and holiday specials, polls ("which one tonight?").
- Never: stock photos of dishes they don't sell. Prices only from the user.
- CTA: order on WhatsApp / the app / call; delivery areas.

**Home and business services** (cleaning, pest control, repairs, laundry, moving)
- Say: the result (a clean home), trust (trained crew, safe products), convenience (pick-up, booking by WhatsApp).
- Series: the full launch set — teasers, countdown, services, how we work, tips, neighbourhoods, landmarks of the
  city, engagement, boarding-pass launch metaphors.
- Pictures: the service as a product illustration with hands; the crew at work in local scenes; before/after.
- CTA: "Call to book at" + numbers, handle with icons.

**Health, clinics, pharmacy, wellness, fitness**
- Say: care, access, appointment ease, prevention tips. Calm colourways.
- Never: diagnose, promise cures, before/after bodies for medical claims, real patient faces without consent.
- Series: services, book an appointment, health tips (tip layout), myth vs fact (split), meet the team, health days.

**Education, edtech, children's apps, schools**
- Say: progress, fun, safety, what the child learns. Speak to parents; show children's world.
- Series: what they learn (one topic a post), the mascot's moments (character peeking in from an edge), parent tips,
  progress/levels, release notes, quotes from parents, holidays.
- Never: children's faces from stock as if they're users; for faith content, ask what may not be depicted.

**Retail, fashion, beauty, e-commerce**
- Say: the product, the look, the drop, the price. Photo-led.
- Series: new in, product heroes, lookbook (frame), how to wear/use (tip), sale (countdown, mega %), reviews, gift
  guides, last chance.

**SaaS, B2B, tools**
- Say: the problem, the outcome, the proof (numbers, logos), the feature in one line.
- Series: feature drops (release), problem/solution (split), stats (mega), customer quotes, how-tos, events/webinars.
- Pictures: product UI in device frames; abstract shapes in brand colours.

**Real estate, property**: listings (photo full, ticket-style fact strips), neighbourhoods (places), process steps,
mortgage tips (with the finance rules above), open-house countdown.

**Events, conferences, churches/mosques, communities, NGOs**: save the date, speakers/programme, countdown, venue and
logistics, live day, thank you, impact numbers (NGO), volunteer calls. Respect what the community won't depict.

**Transport, logistics, travel, hospitality**: routes and places, booking, timetables (ticket layouts suit them),
safety, seasonal trips, reviews.

If the industry isn't listed: take the closest one, keep the six answers, and say which you based it on.

## 5. Copy

- Headlines 3–8 words, one idea, one key word in `[brackets]`. Speak to the reader ("your money", "your home").
- Local and specific beats generic: the city, its areas, its landmarks, its holidays, its slang if the brand uses it.
- Sentence case. No numbered lists. No exclamation marks in headlines. No "Unlock", "Elevate", "Seamless",
  "Revolutionize", "In today's world" — they read as generated.
- Every post has a caption: the line restated warmly, one useful detail, the call, then hashtags from brand.json.
- Write a different sentence for every post in a series; never swap one word in a template sentence.

## 6. Hand back

A plan the user can scan before 100 posts exist: the six answers, the series with counts and layouts, ten sample
headlines, and the list of facts you need from them. Then build.
